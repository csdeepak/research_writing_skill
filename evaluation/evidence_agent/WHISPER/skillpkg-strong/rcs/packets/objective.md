In the authors' words, this paper is meant to show that a single speech recognition and translation model, trained purely by scaling up weakly supervised (audio, transcript) data collected from the internet, can generalize in a zero-shot setting — with no self-supervised pretraining step and no fine-tuning on any target dataset — across a wide range of datasets, languages, and acoustic conditions. A reader should come away understanding that this scaling approach substantially closes the gap between machine and human transcription accuracy and sets a new reported translation result, that the benefit of more training data levels off well before the full dataset is used, and that multilingual recognition, language identification, and translation on the highest-resource languages still lag behind specialized supervised systems trained specifically for those benchmarks.
