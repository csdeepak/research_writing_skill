In the authors' words, this paper is meant to show that scaling weakly supervised, multitask, multilingual training data for speech — audio paired with text gathered automatically from the internet, rather than hand-transcribed — to an unprecedented size (680,000 hours) produces a single speech recognition and translation model that generalizes zero-shot, without any fine-tuning, across many datasets, languages, and acoustic conditions. The paper asks whether this kind of scale, which has already been used for unsupervised audio pre-training and, to a lesser degree, for pooling existing supervised datasets, can be pushed further on the supervision side itself to close the gap between benchmark performance and real-world robustness. A reader should come away understanding: what data and model choices produced this result; where the resulting model (Whisper) is measurably more robust than same-benchmark-trained systems and where it still falls short of specialized supervised systems; how performance scales with both training-data quantity and model size; and what open questions (language coverage, the source of robustness, fine-tuning) remain unresolved.
