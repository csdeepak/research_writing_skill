# Paper Spine

1. **Problem.** Speech recognition systems that reach human-level accuracy on individual benchmarks (e.g., LibriSpeech) still generalize poorly when moved to new datasets, domains, or noise conditions, because they are fine-tuned to the fine-grained style of one training distribution. [C003, C006]
2. **Gap.** Prior large-scale approaches address this either by pre-training encoders on unlabeled audio without supervision (leaving no decoder/language model and requiring fine-tuning for each new setting) or by combining a modest number of existing supervised datasets; neither has scaled weakly-supervised, weakly-curated supervision itself as the main lever. [C001, C014]
3. **Question.** Does scaling weakly supervised, multitask, multilingual training data (transcripts and translations gathered from the internet, not human gold-labeled corpora) to an unprecedented size produce a single speech model that generalizes zero-shot, without dataset-specific fine-tuning? [C001]
4. **Approach.** Train one encoder–decoder Transformer, "Whisper," directly to predict transcripts/translations/timestamps as text (with light-weight, largely automated filtering rather than manual curation) on 680,000 hours of multilingual and multitask audio-text pairs collected from the internet, then evaluate it zero-shot — with no fine-tuning — on many external benchmarks. [C001, C007]
5. **Key finding.** The zero-shot Whisper models approach human-level robustness and accuracy: they reduce error by 55.2% on average relative to a supervised LibriSpeech baseline across 12 diverse datasets, reach a new zero-shot state of the art of 29.1 BLEU on X→en speech translation, and are more noise-robust than 14 LibriSpeech-trained baselines, while performance scales predictably with both training-data quantity and model size. [C003, C004, C005, C006, C007]
6. **Meaning.** Robustness in speech recognition can come from the *breadth and diversity* of weakly supervised pre-training data rather than from careful in-domain fine-tuning, suggesting this scaling strategy — long used in NLP — has been underappreciated in speech research. [C015, C010]
7. **Main limit.** The training data is English-heavy (65% English) with a long tail of low-resource languages, the correlation between more data and better outcomes is far weaker for translation (r²=0.24) than for recognition (r²=0.83), and Whisper still underperforms specialized supervised systems on some benchmarks (VoxPopuli, Fleurs language ID). [C014, C018, C016, C020]
