In the authors' words, this paper is meant to present OpenHands, an open, MIT-licensed platform
that packages the infrastructure an AI agent needs to act on real digital tasks (an "agent hub" of
implemented agents plus an evaluation framework integrating 15 established benchmarks across
software-engineering, web-interaction, and miscellaneous-assistance tasks), and to report what
happens when one general-purpose agent, run without per-domain modification, is evaluated across
that full suite. A reader should come away understanding what the platform provides, how one
unmodified agent performed across three very different kinds of digital tasks (doing well relative
to baselines on several benchmarks while trailing specialized or trained systems on others), and
exactly how far that mixed pattern can be trusted given that every reported number is a single run
with no variance and two pairs of numbers conflict in the source material.
