## 30-second outline (paper spine)

- 1. **Problem.** In a team of LLM agents that share a semantic memory, every query can be sent to all agents (global search), which is costly in tokens; deciding which agent to ask is the problem ASMOS addresses. {C032} {C033}
- 2. **Gap.** Global search consults every candidate (a mean candidate-set size of 32.0 in the test corpus), and whether ownership learned from verified outcomes can cut that cost without losing answerability has not been measured; no verified literature is available, so related work is marked as missing. {C033}
- 3. **Question.** Does routing queries to a learned owner cut LLM token cost relative to global search, what does it do to accuracy and answerability, is the saving tied to ownership evolving, and how does ownership behave when a new topic appears? {C001} {C007} {C005} {C009}
- 4. **Approach.** Compare four routing arms (global search, learned ownership, ownership frozen at its prior, similarity routing without ownership) on a constructed 50-query corpus over 10 seeds, with a bootstrap interval and a one-sided signed-rank test, then test new-topic adaptation and a static question-answering task. {C032} {C004} {C009} {C013}
- 5. **Key finding.** On the constructed corpus, learned-ownership routing used 22.1% fewer LLM tokens per query than global search (95% CI 17.9% to 26.3%), while answering slightly fewer questions correctly in every measured seed. {C001} {C002} {C007}
- 6. **Meaning.** The saving is consistent with ownership evolution being what produces it in this corpus, and ASMOS adapts to a new topic with no retraining, but it is a routing and cost tool and not a question-answering method. {C005} {C009} {C015}
- 7. **Main limit.** The corpus is deliberately constructed, so the size of the saving is not a rate for organic multi-agent workloads; accuracy is lower under routing, and the causal reading rests on one ablation. {L004} {L012} {L013}

## Findings in one sentence each

- ASMOS (Adaptive Semantic Memory Operating System) is a shared semantic-memory layer for teams of LLM agents plus an ownership layer that decides which agent to ask: ownership of a topic is learned online from verification-gated reputation (a verified claim raises an agent's ownership of a topic, a refuted claim lowers it), and a query is routed to the learned owner, the router scoring similarity times ownership with a threshold-gated (tau) fallback to global search. {C032}
- On the constructed asymmetric two-agent corpus, mean context tokens per query were 123.2 (A0), 83.6 (A1), 122.3 (A2) and 65.9 (A3), and the mean candidate-set size was 32.0, 20.8, 31.7 and 17.9 for the same four arms; the evaluation set has five query classes. {C033}
- On the constructed 50-query transactive corpus (gpt-4o-mini, 10 seeds), transactive routing (A1) used 22.1% fewer LLM total tokens per query than global search (A0), a mean of 39.8 fewer tokens per query, with a bootstrap 95% CI over queries of 17.9% to 26.3% and per-seed reductions between 21.9% and 22.2%. {C001}
- In the five newest seeds (66, 77, 88, 99, 110), answer accuracy was 0.72 for A0 and A2 in every seed, between 0.68 and 0.70 for A1 (below A0 in every seed) and between 0.54 and 0.56 for A3; no paired test of the accuracy difference is available and the grading procedure is not documented. {C007}
- The token saving is consistent with ownership evolution being what produces it in this corpus: with ownership frozen at the prior (A2) the router falls back to global search (route@1 0.0) and recovers almost none of the saving. {C005}
- In the new-topic regime with the all-mpnet-base-v2 embedder (5 seeds, 10 steps, 5 evaluation queries per step), ASMOS reached a cumulative regret of 3.24 (std 1.30) with 0 retrains and routed the new topic in 5 of 5 seeds; a frozen classifier never routed it (regret 10.0), a classifier retrained every 5 steps had regret 4.8 (std 0.4, 2 retrains) and one retrained every 10 steps had regret 9.0 (1 retrain); the artifact also records 10 labels consumed by ASMOS, as by the retraining classifiers; the definition of that counter is not documented. {C009}
- Across 50 queries and 10 seeds the mean LLM tokens per query were 180.2 for A0, 140.4 for A1, 179.3 for A2 (ownership frozen at the prior) and 123.4 for A3 (similarity routing without ownership); A2 is within 0.9 tokens of A0 and 38.9 tokens above A1. {C004}
- On 24 question-answering items from the RULER benchmark (gpt-4o-mini, single run), exact-match scores were 0.3333 for No-Memory, 0.7083 for RAG, 0.1667 for ASMOS-memory and 0.625 for ASMOS+RAG (equal to the containment scores in every row), with token-F1 of 0.5642, 0.7513, 0.4155 and 0.4329. {C013}
- A one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 50 per-query token differences between A0 and A1 gave W = 1141.5 and p = 6.788e-09 (48 non-zero pairs of 50); the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation is r = 0.941 (T+ = 1141.5, T- = 34.5, z = 5.679). {C002}
- On this static single-agent span-retrieval question-answering task ASMOS-memory alone scored below No-Memory and below RAG, and ASMOS did not beat RAG; the authors state that ASMOS is not a question-answering-accuracy method. {C015}
