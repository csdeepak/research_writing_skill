Intended readers: machine-learning researchers from other subfields (adjacent researchers) --
people who know general ML, deep learning, standard evaluation practice, and statistics, but not
this project's subfield-specific terminology, datasets, or prior work.

They can be assumed to know: supervised learning; train/test splits and held-out evaluation;
standard accuracy metrics and the idea of a ROC/AUC curve; convolutional and fully-connected
network basics; quantization as a general concept (that lower-precision numerics exist); the
general idea of a community benchmark suite.

They cannot be assumed to know: TinyML as a named field and its resource envelope;
microcontroller-class (MCU) hardware constraints; post-training quantization (PTQ) versus
quantization-aware training (QAT) as specific terms; closed/open submission divisions as this
suite defines them; the specific prior benchmarks discussed (CoreMark, MLMark, MLPerf Inference)
and how they differ; the four component datasets and reference models and why each was chosen.

Binding reader types for this review:
- A (specialist in this exact subfield): present, but not the primary target.
- B (adjacent researcher, different subfield, same broad field): the primary target.
- E (mixed venue, layered readership): present as a secondary check.
