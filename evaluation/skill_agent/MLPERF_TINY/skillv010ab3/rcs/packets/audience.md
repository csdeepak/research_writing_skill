Intended readers: machine-learning researchers who work in other subfields (for example vision, language or theory) and want to understand what MLPerf Tiny is and what its first round showed.
They can be assumed to know: general machine learning and deep learning, common architectures (CNN, ResNet, MobileNet, autoencoder), train/validation/test practice, accuracy, top-1 and AUC, quantization in general, latency and throughput, benchmarks in general.
They cannot be assumed to know: microcontrollers and embedded hardware, TinyML, how energy is measured on devices, TensorFlow Lite Micro, the specific datasets (Speech Commands, Visual Wake Words, ToyADMOS/DCASE), earlier benchmarks such as CoreMark, MLMark and MLPerf inference.
Binding reader types for this review: A (specialist, checks precision), B (adjacent researcher, main audience), E (mixed readers needing layered explanation).
Venue type: journal-style research article (venue unspecified).
