In the authors' words, this paper is meant to present OpenHands, a community-developed open platform for building and evaluating AI agents that interact with the world the way a human developer does: by writing code, using a command line and browsing the web. It asks whether one open platform can supply the shared agent abstraction, sandboxed runtime, reusable tools, multi-agent delegation and integrated benchmarks that such agents need, and whether a single generalist agent built on it performs competitively across software-engineering, web-browsing and general-assistance benchmarks. A reader should come away understanding what the platform consists of and why it is designed that way, what evaluation results were reported and against which comparators, and what those results do and do not establish.
