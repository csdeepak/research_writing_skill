Intended readers: machine-learning researchers from other subfields (adjacent researchers) who read this article to learn what the OpenHands project is, how it is built and what its evaluation shows.
They can be assumed to know: general machine learning and deep learning, large language models and prompting (zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought), standard evaluation practice (baselines, success rate, accuracy), basic statistics (variance, seeds, confidence intervals), and general software-engineering vocabulary such as containers, REST APIs and unit tests.
They cannot be assumed to know: the vocabulary of LLM-agent frameworks, the benchmarks used (SWE-Bench and its Lite subset, HumanEvalFix, WebArena, MiniWoB++, GAIA, GPQA, AgentBench, MINT, ProofWriter, Entity Deduction Arena), the CodeAct approach, or the earlier agent frameworks and systems used as comparators.
Binding reader types for this review: A (a specialist in agent systems who wants rigor and a precise account of what is new), B (an adjacent researcher who needs subfield terms defined and the reason for the design), E (a mixed reader who needs each section to open accessibly).
Venue type: conference-style research article (standalone Markdown; tables allowed, no images).
