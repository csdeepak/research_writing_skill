# Paper Spine

Seven lines, each bound to claim/limitation IDs. Audience: mode B (adjacent ML researchers).

1. Problem: Retrieval components are a first step in many NLP systems, but neural retrieval models are usually trained and tested on one dataset, so how they behave outside that single setting is largely unknown {C001}.
2. Gap: The multi-dataset resources that exist before this work test much narrower settings than "broad zero-shot generalization" -- one task and mostly small, Wikipedia-only corpora, or knowledge-intensive tasks that retrieve only from Wikipedia and treat retrieval as secondary {C002}.
3. Question: Which retrieval architectures (lexical, sparse-neural, dense, late-interaction, re-ranking) generalize best in a zero-shot, out-of-domain setting across diverse tasks and domains, at what computational cost, and how much does in-domain accuracy predict this {C003}?
4. Approach: We built BEIR, a benchmark of 18 datasets over 9 tasks selected for diversity of task, domain, difficulty and annotation method, converted to one data format and scored with one metric (nDCG@10), and evaluated 10 public retrieval systems spanning the five architecture families under this common protocol, including a manual case study probing annotation bias on one dataset {C001, C021, C022}.
5. Key finding: In-domain MS MARCO accuracy does not predict zero-shot performance: BM25 trails neural systems by 7-18 points in-domain yet, averaged over the 18 datasets, beats six of the nine neural systems tested {C004, C005, C006}; the two architectures that generalize best (re-ranking, late-interaction) cost far more compute than the rest {C007, C008}; and a manual annotation check on one dataset shows the benchmark's own relevance judgments can be biased toward lexical systems {C018, C019}.
6. Meaning: Good accuracy on the field's dominant single-dataset protocol is a poor proxy for out-of-distribution retrieval behavior, so architectures should be compared on broad, heterogeneous suites and on both accuracy and cost, not on one in-domain leaderboard {C005, C008}.
7. Main limit: The comparison covers 18 English-only, mostly short-document, single-run evaluations of 10 publicly available systems as they existed at the time, with no seed variance reported {L001, L002, L006, L008}, and the annotation-bias finding was demonstrated on one of the eighteen datasets, so how far it generalizes to the rest of the benchmark is not established {L007}.
