# Paper Spine

1. **Problem.** Neural retrieval models are typically trained and evaluated on a single dataset or domain, so it is unclear how well they perform when asked to retrieve for new tasks and domains they were never trained on {C003}.
2. **Gap.** The only existing broad-coverage retrieval evaluations, MultiReQA and KILT, each cover a single task type and lean heavily on Wikipedia-scale corpora, so neither shows how different retrieval *architectures* compare when generalizing zero-shot across many tasks and domains at once {C020}.
3. **Question.** Across diverse retrieval tasks and domains, how well do ten representative systems from five architecture families (lexical, sparse, dense, late-interaction, re-ranking) generalize zero-shot relative to the classical lexical baseline BM25, and does the way existing test collections are built distort that comparison {C001}{C017}?
4. **Approach.** We built BEIR, a benchmark unifying 18 public datasets across 9 retrieval tasks and domains into one data format and one metric (nDCG@10), and evaluated the ten systems on it zero-shot; we then audited one dataset, TREC-COVID, for annotation-pool bias by manually judging each system's previously-unjudged top hits {C021}.
5. **Key finding.** No architecture dominates across all 18 datasets; BM25 remains a strong zero-shot baseline even though neural models beat it in-domain by 7-18 points {C001}{C002}, cross-attention-based re-ranking and late-interaction models generalize best (+11% and +2.5% average over BM25) but are the slowest and most memory-hungry {C004}{C005}{C015}, while several dense and term-reweighting sparse models underperform BM25 on average (down to -47.7%) {C006}{C008}; separately, lexically-pooled annotation understates non-lexical systems' true performance by up to several points of nDCG@10 {C017}{C018}.
6. **Meaning.** In-domain accuracy is a poor proxy for out-of-domain robustness, and the mechanism most associated with robust generalization here is cross-attention(-like) query-document interaction rather than embedding architecture alone {C003}{C019}; some of the apparent weakness of non-lexical systems is itself an artifact of how test collections are annotated {C018}.
7. **Main limit.** These conclusions are drawn from English-only, single/dual-field, short-to-medium-length (mostly under a few hundred words) text retrieval without auxiliary ranking signals, without error bars on the individual comparisons, and the annotation-bias finding was measured on one dataset {L001}{L002}{L003}{L004}{L006}{L008}.
