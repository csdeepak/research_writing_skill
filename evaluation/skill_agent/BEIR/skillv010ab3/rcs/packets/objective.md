In the authors' words, this paper is meant to present a benchmark for evaluating retrieval models when no training data exist for the target task, and a comparison of ten retrieval systems from five architecture families on it. It asks whether accuracy on the training dataset predicts accuracy on new tasks and domains, which kinds of systems generalize best and at what computational cost, and whether the human relevance labels in existing datasets treat lexical and non-lexical systems fairly. A reader should come away understanding what the benchmark contains and how it is scored, what the comparison found and how far those findings can be trusted, and why the way relevance labels are collected can change conclusions about retrieval systems.
